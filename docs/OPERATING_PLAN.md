# From a working app to a business

## The offer

**Discover useful and surprising products, try them, and meet the people building them.**

The user requested all audiences: creators/freelancers, small businesses, and developers. The catalog supports each. Content should use recognisable series so the audience for each video is clear. Compare outcomes by series rather than assuming a general audience will behave as one group.

The public site and Instagram page serve different jobs. Videos earn attention; the site helps people find the exact product, save discoveries, explore related tools, and join a conversation. New viewers, returning visitors, product users, and paying customers are different measurements.

## A repeatable publishing operation

| Stage           | Automated in this build                                          | Editorial or setup work                                                 |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Product intake  | Persist submissions and route to review                          | Recruit makers and gather product evidence                              |
| Curation        | Filter by audience and category                                  | Decide whether a product is useful, accurate, and worth featuring       |
| Drafting        | Prepare script, caption, shot list from submitted facts          | Check facts, select a concrete demonstration, improve the hook          |
| Demo production | Render supplied MP4 into a branded portrait video                | Obtain/record real product footage; record voiceover if desired         |
| Approval        | Enforce state and source/rights requirements                     | Review the final content                                                |
| Publishing      | Persistent queue, cap, processing poll, official API publication | Connect account, manage credentials, investigate uncertain outcomes     |
| Measurement     | Visits, repeat visitors, saves, product-link clicks, sources     | Interpret the evidence; ask makers about signups and sales              |
| Monetisation    | Capture inquiries and support paid-feature labels                | Agree deliverables, collect payment, fulfil/refund, and report honestly |

The goal is to automate reliable repetition. Generating more content does not by itself establish demand, credibility, or a profitable offer.

These are content briefs, not ready-to-publish claims about untested products. Use actual footage and name the featured maker when chosen.

Batch preparation can reduce context switching: gather product/source details together, record demos together, review the week's videos together, then schedule. Keep enough reviewed material queued to sustain the chosen cadence. Test daily posting for a bounded period rather than treating frequency as proof of progress.

## First 30 days

### Days 1–7: establish a trustworthy starting collection

- Use the working local site to collect feedback, not as evidence of traction.
- Recruit a small set of genuine makers and ask for authorised demo assets and source facts.
- Publish original demonstrations. Mention a concrete use case and any meaningful limitation you observed.
- Connect the bio link to `/?from=instagram&utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=organic_social&utm_campaign=pilot`.
- Collect voluntary comments on what viewers wanted to find and whether a product helped.

### Days 8–21: observe the whole journey

- Review video performance in Instagram. Its Insights API is not connected in this build.
- Measure site arrivals, product-link clicks, saves, and visits on later days. Compare per series.
- Ask participating makers whether referred visitors signed up or became paying customers. Track only what can actually be attributed.
- Keep producing formats that lead to useful actions. A widely watched video with few interested visitors may be entertainment success but does not yet validate the platform.
- Investigate where people stop: weak click-through, an unhelpful destination, poor product fit, or no reason to return require different changes.

### Days 22–30: test a paid offer

Two distinct experiments are available:

1. **Production service:** a maker pays for a clearly scoped demo video package they can use. This can have value before the platform has a large audience. Specify deliverables, revision limits, rights, and timing. Any sponsored editorial placement is disclosed.
2. **Sponsored distribution:** once the page can show relevant audience results, offer a labelled placement plus a report of observed reach and clicks. Do not guarantee customer acquisition from unproven conversion data.

Price after estimating editing/review time, fulfilment work, software costs, and the maker's willingness to pay. An inquiry is not an order; an order is not profit. Record collected revenue and direct costs separately when payment handling is added.

Affiliate revenue can be explored only for actual programmes whose terms fit the product and audience. Disclose the relationship. Do not rank a product as better merely because it pays more.

## The numbers that make the decision clearer

| Question                                       | Evidence                                                                                                         |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Do people enjoy the content?                   | Repeat viewers, meaningful shares/saves, and comments in Instagram                                               |
| Does it bring relevant people to the platform? | Tagged arrivals and product-link clicks, by content series                                                       |
| Does the site have its own usefulness?         | Repeat visits, saved collections, conversations, and users returning to a product                                |
| Does it help makers?                           | Attributed signups/activation/purchases, when makers can verify them                                             |
| Can it earn money?                             | An independently paying customer, delivered work, collected revenue                                              |
| Can it be profitable?                          | Revenue minus direct production, distribution, payment, hosting, and support costs, including the founder's time |

No universal benchmark or growth forecast is assumed. Pick a baseline and decision rules before each experiment. The first milestones should be real outsiders returning, a maker receiving useful users, and a customer paying for a delivered result. A large daily audience is a later outcome to earn, not a property that automation can install.

- [Meta's official Instagram API collection](https://www.postman.com/meta/instagram/documentation/6yqw8pt/instagram-api): professional accounts, Facebook Login permissions, container creation, status polling, publication.
- [Instagram Trial Reels](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/12/trial-reels-try-content-non-followers-first-see-what-perfoms-best/): testing content with non-followers. This is a native Instagram option, not an integrated feature of this app.
- Product facts in the starter catalog link directly to the maker's official site. Review those sources again before making a video.

No source establishes that this specific business will achieve a given audience or revenue.
